# German Currywurst

### Ingredients

- Sausages (German Wiener, tradiionally)
- Tomato sauce
- Baking soda (very little)
- Curry powder
- Smoked paprika
- Cayenne pepper
- Worcestershire sauce
- Beef broth

### Instructions

- Put the sausages in a pot of cold water and bring it to boil. Remove once cooked.

- [Adjust the portion to fit your taste] Heat up a sauce pan and add tomato sauce and a tiny bit of baking soda (to reduce the acidity). Then season it with curry powder, smoked paprika, cayenne pepper, and onion powder. Add Worcestershire sauce and beef broth to adjust the viscocity.

- Cut up the cooked sausages into bite size and generously coat them with the sauce on top.

### Background

This is a common street food in Berlin. After World War 2, German was in some kind of food scarcity. the American and British soliders that still remain provided two ingredients: tomato sauce (US), and curry powder and Worcestershire sauce (UK, which owned India back then).


# Ghackets mit Hörnli (Swiss Macaroni with Meat Sauce)

### Ingredients

- Macaroni
- Olive oil
- Minced beef
- Tomato paste
- Shredded cheese
- Apples

### Instructions

- Cook the macaroni per the instructions on the package.

- Heat up a pan and add some olive oil. Stir fry the minced beef. Once cooked, add tomato paste and stir for a minute. The add some pasta water to make it into a sauce. Don't make it too watery! Season it with some salt if necessary.

- Put the macaroni onto a plate and top it off with the sauce. Add some shredded cheese on top.

- Peel an apple or two and remove the core. Put it into the blender to make an apple puree. Serve it on the side. (You can add some lemmon juice to keep it from oxidizing.)

### Background

This is a very traditional family dish in Switzerland, but you may have a hard time finding it in restairants in Switzerland. It is basically the Swiss version of a Bolognese sauce. But unlike Bolognese suace, efficiency is what makes this dish stands out.
